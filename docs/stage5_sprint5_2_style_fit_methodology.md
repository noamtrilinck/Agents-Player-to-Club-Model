## Part A — Exact meaning of Stage 4 profile types

### OBSERVED

Built by `build_observed_club_position_evidence.py::build_profiles`. For a given (club,
position): take every eligible player-season row that played that position at that club
(`club_position_player_evidence.csv`), and compute the **minutes-weighted mean** of each of the
11 Stage 3 CORE Ability Final (Competitive-Context-adjusted) T-scores across those
player-seasons (`_weighted_mean`, `np.average(..., weights=positional_minutes)`).

- **What contributes**: any player-season at that exact (club_id, position_group), any season
  within the eligible universe — not restricted to the current season.
- **Aggregation**: minutes-weighted mean, per dimension independently (a player missing one
  CORE dimension is simply excluded from that dimension's mean via `values.notna()`, not
  imputed — `observed_<dim>_n_players` discloses how many players contributed to each
  dimension).
- **Minutes matter**: yes, directly — weight, not just an eligibility filter.
- **Sample size matters**: not to the mean itself, but is fully disclosed alongside it
  (`n_contributing_players`, `total_positional_minutes`, `primary_player_share`,
  `top2_player_share`) — confirmed 45.8% of combos are a single contributor (Sprint 5.1
  finding), so the "mean" frequently **is** one player's own profile.
- **Team Environment**: does not enter this calculation at all.
- **Prediction/modeling**: none. Confirmed by the module docstring itself ("no clustering, no
  archetypes, no System Compatibility... this script computes nothing beyond a minutes-weighted
  average"). **OBSERVED is purely descriptive** — a direct empirical record of who has actually
  played there, never a target or recruitment ideal.

### System-Compatible / predicted profile

Built by `build_system_compatible_profiles.py`. Position-specific **Ridge regression**:

- **Target**: the OBSERVED profile above (`observed_<dim>` columns, i.e. Sprint 4.2's
  minutes-weighted means), for every club that has direct positional evidence at that position.
- **Input**: the club's own RAW Team Environment (30 CORE features,
  `locked_team_environment_features.py`) — club-level, no position dimension.
- **Training set**: only clubs **with** direct Sprint 4.2 evidence at that position (the model
  cannot train where there's no target). 9 positions use an independent Ridge per position
  (alpha 100 for Centre Back, 300 for the other 8); Right/Left Midfielder use a pooled Ridge
  with the same-flank Back + Winger positions (position-encoded, alpha 300) — the Sprint 4.6
  evidence-based fallback for these two WEAK-tier positions.
- **Prediction step**: once fit, the model predicts a profile for **every** candidate club
  (all 513), including clubs with zero direct evidence — this is what makes "fully inferred"
  rows (1,581 of 5,643) possible at all: their `predicted_<dim>` values come purely from that
  club's own Team Environment run through the position's learned Team-Environment→profile
  relationship, never from any player who's played there.
- **How Team Environment affects it**: it is the *only* input feature set.
- **How observed player evidence affects it, for a club that has evidence**: **only indirectly**
  — that club's own (club, position) observed profile was one training data point among many
  (pooled, club-grouped-CV-validated) used to fit the position's Ridge weights. The prediction
  for that specific club is **not** a blend of "this club's own observed evidence" with the
  model — it is the model's output for that club's Team Environment, exactly as it would be for
  any other club. `observed_<dim>` is carried alongside purely for comparison/validation, in a
  separate column family, never merged into `predicted_<dim>` at this stage.
- **Does the final production profile blend observed evidence and prediction? Only for the 255
  qualifying two-profile cases** (Sprint 4.8) — see ALTERNATIVE below. For the other 5,388
  Club×Position combinations (the overwhelming majority, including most evidence-bearing ones),
  **`predicted_<dim>` is the raw, unblended Ridge output** — confirmed directly from
  `build_multi_profile_extension.py`: `base["profile_id"] = "A"` copies `single` (the pure
  Ridge file) verbatim; the 70/30 blend line only executes inside the `for _, row in
  qualifying.iterrows()` loop.

### ALTERNATIVE archetype

Built by `build_multi_profile_extension.py`, for exactly the 255 Club×Position combinations
that pass the locked Sprint 4.8 "R2_moderate" eligibility rule (second contributor's minute
share ≥30%, total positional minutes ≥1,800, learnable-dimension distance between the top-two
contributors ≥1.5× the position's own homogeneous-case median — **and only where direct Sprint
4.2 evidence exists at all**; fully-inferred combos can never qualify).

- **Why it exists**: evidence that a position at that club has genuinely been filled by two
  distinguishable player archetypes, not incremental variation around one type.
- **Detection**: two-cluster nearest-centroid assignment over the position's contributing
  players' own Stage 3 CORE vectors, seeded by the two highest-minute-share players
  (`assign_clusters` — one pass, not iterative k-means).
- **Football meaning**: the club has genuinely used two different *kinds* of player at this
  slot with materially different underlying profiles (e.g. a defensive-first Right Back vs. an
  attacking-overlap Right Back) — not "a slightly different level of the same type."
- **Mathematical construction** (both A and B, for a qualifying case): `0.30 × Ridge prediction
  + 0.70 × that cluster's own minutes-weighted Stage 3 mean`. This **overwrites** the PRIMARY
  row's `predicted_<dim>` for that Club×Position (it is no longer the pure unblended Ridge
  output once a case qualifies) and creates a second row (`profile_id="B"`,
  `profile_type=ALTERNATIVE`) with the same blend applied to cluster B. Confirmed empirically
  (§4.2 of the canonical doc): the 30% Ridge anchor is there specifically to prevent Profile A/B
  from being literal single-player copies (median distance from the raw seed player rises from
  ≈0 unblended to a median of 9.0 at 30%).
- **Equally valid, or secondary?** Diagnostics in Part E below (255 real cases, full candidate
  population) show Profile B is the *better* fit for roughly half of all realistic candidates on
  average — **not secondary evidence**. Treat both as equally legitimate ways of fitting the
  club, never as primary + fallback.
- **Important caveat discovered while tracing the code**: for a qualifying two-profile
  Club×Position, the row's `observed_<dim>` field still reflects the **whole-position, both-
  clusters-combined** Sprint 4.2 mean (unchanged from before Sprint 4.8) — it is not
  per-cluster. A future Observed Fit calculation for a two-profile club therefore has no
  per-archetype Observed counterpart to compare against; only `predicted_<dim>` (System-
  Compatible) is meaningfully split A vs. B. This constrains Part B's answer below.

## Part B — Which compatibility components genuinely exist

### Observed Fit — **legitimate, with a scope restriction**

Yes: comparing a player's CORE vector to `observed_<dim>` is a well-defined, real signal for
the 4,062 of 5,643 Club×Position combinations that have direct evidence
(`has_observed_evidence=True`). It answers "does this player resemble who has actually played
here" — descriptive similarity, not a modeled requirement. **It must never be computed for
fully-inferred combos** (`observed_<dim>` is null there by construction — 1,581 of 5,643). For
the 255 two-profile combos, Observed Fit can only be computed against the combined,
both-archetypes profile (see the caveat above) — it cannot currently be split into an
"Observed Fit to Archetype A" vs "B" the way System Fit can.

### System Compatibility — **legitimate, this is `predicted_<dim>`**

Yes: `predicted_<dim>` is exactly the Stage 4 output built to answer "what does this club's
playing environment predict it should need at this position" — the Team-Environment-driven,
generalized relationship, independent of which specific players happen to be there today
(except for the 255 blended cases, where it's still generalized but anchored 70% to the
observed archetype). This is available for **all** 5,643 combos (both fully-inferred and
evidence-bearing), for both PRIMARY and ALTERNATIVE profiles.

### Squad Complementarity — **recommendation: defer to a later stage, do not build a placeholder that implies it exists**

Sprint 5.1 already confirmed Stage 4 builds nothing resembling this concept — no code, no
column, no intermediate artifact anywhere in the four Stage 4 sprints. Nothing in Stage 4's
outputs (Observed, predicted, reliability, or evidence-depth fields) can be reinterpreted as
Squad Complementarity without fabricating meaning that isn't there — e.g. `observed_<dim>` is
descriptive evidence, not "what the squad currently lacks," and using distance-from-observed as
a complementarity proxy would silently conflate it with Observed Fit (the opposite signal:
Observed Fit rewards similarity to the incumbent, Squad Complementarity is supposed to reward
**difference** from the incumbent in a *useful* direction — these are not interchangeable, and
the roadmap explicitly warns against combining them).

**Recommendation: option 2 — build it later as a genuinely new, separate squad-analysis
component**, not simultaneously with Sprint 5.2/5.3's fit engine and not by removing the concept
from the roadmap entirely (option 1 would silently drop a deliverable the roadmap explicitly
promised architecture for). It requires its own data (which players currently occupy each
slot, not just an aggregate profile — `club_position_player_evidence.csv` already retains this
detail, confirmed preserved specifically for this purpose per Sprint 4.2's own doc) and its own
definition of "useful difference," which is a distinct methodological question from style fit
and shouldn't be rushed to fit Sprint 5.2's timeline. Do not schedule it inside Stage 5's
remaining sprints by default — flagging it as a Stage 6/7-adjacent question for you to place
explicitly, per the roadmap's own three-way split (A/B/C in Stage 4's design docs).

## Part C — Candidate similarity methods (tested on the real population)

Computed for all 7,467 representative players against every candidate PRIMARY profile at their
own position, self-club excluded (3,823,104 valid pairs). Population-level per-dimension
statistics (used only for the *statistically justified* weighting in method 3, never a
subjective choice):

| Dimension | mean | std |
|---|---|---|
| crossing_wide_delivery | 49.70 | 7.37 |
| finishing_shot_threat | 49.99 | 7.36 |
| progressive_passing | 49.98 | 8.74 |
| chance_creation | 49.61 | 6.29 |
| ball_retention_security | 49.99 | 6.91 |
| build_up_involvement | 49.98 | 9.50 |
| long_distribution | 50.00 | 6.82 |
| ball_carrying_dribbling | 49.94 | 6.87 |
| defensive_ball_winning | 49.96 | 9.12 |
| ground_duels_physical_contests | 50.03 | 6.94 |
| aerial_duels | 49.99 | 7.75 |

Dimension coverage is excellent: median 11/11 CORE dimensions used per pair for every position;
worst position (Centre Back) still has a 10th-percentile of 10/11 dims — partial-coverage pairs
are the exception, not the norm (consistent with Stage 3's 4.3% missing-any-CORE-dimension
finding).

### 1. Mean Absolute Distance (MAD)
Pooled mean 6.30 T-score points, median 6.01, p10–p90 = 4.02–8.96. Simple, interpretable
("on average, X points off"), robust to a single extreme dimension dominating the score.

### 2. Euclidean distance
Reported normalized by √(dims used) so differing coverage counts stay comparable
(`euclidean_norm`): pooled mean 7.77, median 7.40. Highly correlated with MAD (rank agreement
ρ=0.91 — see Part F) but penalizes concentrated multi-dimension deficits more than MAD's
average does; the two rarely disagree in practice.

### 3. Standardized (statistically-weighted) Euclidean
Weight = 1/population-std per dimension — a real statistical basis (equalizes each axis's
contribution relative to how much that dimension actually varies in the population), not a
hand-picked weight. Pooled mean 3.36 (different scale by construction — not comparable in
absolute terms to raw Euclidean). Rank agreement with MAD: ρ=0.91 — essentially the same
information once population variance is this uniform across dimensions (7.4–9.5 std across all
11 CORE dims is a narrow range to begin with) — **standardization buys very little here**
because the dimensions are already fairly comparable in scale. It would matter more if a future
feature set had dimensions on wildly different scales; the 11 CORE T-scores don't.

### 4. Cosine similarity — confirmed conceptually broken on raw T-scores, fixed by centering
**Raw cosine (uncentered) is nearly useless**: measured directly (Centre Back, Centre Forward),
mean 0.989, std 0.007, range 0.915–0.9999 across the ENTIRE population of pairs — every
player-club pair looks "99% similar" regardless of actual football difference, because all
vectors are dominated by the shared ~50-point offset common to every T-score. This confirms the
exact concern flagged in the brief: raw cosine on T-scores centered at 50 (not 0) degenerates
toward measuring the constant component, not real shape.
**Mean-centered cosine (subtract 50 from every dimension) is meaningfully discriminative**:
pooled mean ≈0.01 (expected — deviations from 50 point in roughly random directions on
average across the whole population), std with real spread (p10=−0.48, p90=+0.50). Centering is
required and materially changes the metric's behavior — do not use raw cosine.
Rank agreement with MAD is the weakest of the "distance-like" methods (ρ=0.79 after sign-flip)
— cosine (even centered) measures something meaningfully *different* from MAD: direction/shape
rather than absolute level. See the Aaron Morley case in Part F for why this matters and can be
actively misleading alone.

### 5. Correlation / shape similarity
Pearson r across the 11 shared dimensions per pair. Weakest agreement with MAD of all tested
methods (ρ=0.66, the most different information content). Mathematically interesting — it
answers "does this player's relative strengths/weaknesses pattern match the club's" — but
**not football-safe as a standalone measure**: it is blind to absolute level entirely, so a
player uniformly 20 points below a demanding profile in the *same shape* scores as a "shape
match" as a player who's actually at the right level. Useful only as a secondary/explanatory
signal ("this player's strengths align with what this role rewards"), never alone.

### 6. Percentile/rank-based distance
Not separately re-tested as a distinct production candidate: given the 11 CORE dimensions are
already T-scores (population mean ≈50, std within a narrow 6.3–9.5 band, no extreme outliers or
skew found in Stage 3's own data-quality audit), converting to population percentile ranks
first would be a monotonic transform very close to a linear rescaling for this specific data —
expected to track raw-T-score MAD/Euclidean closely. Not run as a full separate population pass
given this expectation and Sprint 5.2's time budget; flagged as a cheap follow-up if a future
robustness concern arises (e.g. if a much larger/noisier feature set is added later), not
adopted or rejected outright here.

## Part D — Deficits vs. surpluses (tested empirically)

Three explicit variants tested against the symmetric MAD baseline, over the full population:

- **Asymmetric (deficit ×2, surplus ×1)** — `asym_mad_deficit2x`. Pooled mean 9.44 (vs. MAD's
  6.30 — meaningfully larger whenever real deficits exist, identical to MAD when a player has no
  deficits at all). Rank agreement with MAD: ρ=0.89 — close but not identical; the two disagree
  most exactly where the brief predicted they would (deficit-heavy pairs).
- **Hybrid (deficit ×1, surplus ×0.4)** — `hybrid_mad`. Pooled mean 4.40 (lower than MAD, since
  surplus is discounted rather than penalized). Rank agreement ρ=0.83.
- **Requirement-satisfaction (deficit beyond a 2-point tolerance penalized linearly, surplus
  never penalized)** — `satisfaction_penalty`. Pooled mean 2.18, but by far the **least stable**
  agreement with MAD (ρ=0.56, std 0.35 — the widest and least reliable of any method tested).
  This method effectively ignores how much a player exceeds a requirement and ignores small
  deficits under the tolerance entirely, producing many near-ties among genuinely different
  players — too coarse to recommend as the primary signal, though it may be a reasonable
  **secondary threshold check** ("does this player clear the bar on every dimension") layered on
  top of a continuous score.

### Worked examples (real data, not hypothetical)

**Deficit-dominated case** — Thibaut Van Acker vs. PSV (Central Midfield): MAD=16.97,
deficit-weighted=32.90 (nearly doubles). PSV's CM profile is a demanding two-way box-to-box
requirement (progressive_passing 69, build_up_involvement 67, chance_creation 66,
defensive_ball_winning 52); Van Acker is short on almost every one of those (progressive_passing
−33.6, build_up_involvement −33.7, defensive_ball_winning −27.0), with only two small surpluses
(finishing_shot_threat +7.8, ball_retention_security +3.7). Symmetric and asymmetric methods
**agree on direction** here (both rank him poorly) — the asymmetric method just states the
shortfall more honestly.

**Surplus-dominated case** — Taichi Fukui vs. Kazincbarcika (Central Midfield): every one of
his 11 dimensions is at or above the club's predicted requirement (deltas +0.7 to +8.8, no
deficits at all). MAD and deficit-weighted MAD are **identical** here (3.56 both) — surpluses
never inflate the asymmetric score, confirming the asymmetric method behaves exactly as
intended: it only diverges from symmetric MAD when real deficits exist.

**The football risk case the brief specifically warned about** — Aaron Morley vs. Patro Eisden
Maasmechelen (Central Midfield): MAD ranks this pair 506th of 512 candidates (very poor —
Morley is 9–18 points short on crossing, progressive passing, long distribution, defensive
duels). **Centered cosine ranks the same pair 3rd of 512** — because Morley's relative pattern
(stronger passing/creation dimensions, weaker physical/defensive ones) points in roughly the
same *direction* as the club's demanding, attack-heavy CM requirement, even though he falls well
short of it in absolute terms on most of those same dimensions. **This is exactly the failure
mode the brief anticipated for "shape without level"** — a shape-only or shape-dominant
metric can rank a genuinely underqualified player as an excellent match. It is strong,
data-backed evidence that shape/correlation similarity must never be used as the primary or sole
compatibility signal.

### Recommendation for Part D

**A magnitude-based method (MAD or Euclidean) should be the primary signal, with deficits
weighted more heavily than surpluses** — the asymmetric variant tested here (deficit×2/surplus×1)
is a reasonable, simple starting point that behaves exactly as intended (identical to symmetric
MAD when there's no deficit, meaningfully larger when there is). This is **not** presented as
"the" final weight — 2× is one tested point, not a claim it's optimal; Sprint 5.3 should treat
the deficit/surplus ratio as a tunable parameter validated against real outcomes if/when such
outcomes become available, not a number locked here. **Do not use "higher is always better"
uncritically either**: the data doesn't show large surpluses behaving pathologically in this
sample (Fukui's case, and generally, players with real surpluses aren't penalized under any
tested method) — but a future refinement could examine whether *very* large surpluses (e.g.
+20+ on a single dimension) correlate with genuinely different archetypes rather than "extra
quality," which this sprint did not have grounds to test rigorously with the available data
(would require independent evidence of what "too different" looks like — a question for
football judgment, not purely statistical, and explicitly flagged as unresolved in Part I below).

## Part E — PRIMARY vs. ALTERNATIVE archetype treatment

Diagnostic across all 255 qualifying two-profile Club×Position cases, comparing every candidate
player's MAD fit to Profile A alone vs. Profile B alone vs. best-of-both:

- **Profile B is the better fit for a real player, on average, 51.4% of the time** (mean across
  255 cases; median 49.8%) — essentially split, not a secondary/rare case. In 127 of 255 cases
  (49.8%), Profile B is the better fit for *most* candidates; in only 26 of 255 (10.2%) does B
  rarely win (<20% of candidates).
- **Profiles A and B are genuinely, substantially separated**: mean Euclidean distance between
  the two archetypes' predicted vectors is 31.9 T-score-units (median 31.0, range 18.6–53.7) —
  a large, real separation, not a marginal split.
- Median improvement from taking best-of-two vs. Profile-A-only across the full candidate
  population is small (0.0, because for roughly half of candidates A is already their better
  fit) but the 75th percentile shows a real, non-trivial improvement (median-improvement 75th
  pctile ≈1.4 points) for the players who do benefit from considering B.

### Recommendation for Part E

**Best-fit-to-either-archetype** (`System Fit = best(fit(Player, A), fit(Player, B))`, exactly
as the canonical Stage 4 doc's §7 documented-but-not-implemented semantics already specify) is
the empirically correct treatment — not "primary with alternative as secondary evidence." The
data shows no basis for treating A as more authoritative than B: they're both Ridge-anchored,
both evidence-supported, and each is the better match for roughly half of realistic candidates.
Keeping two separate compatibility scores (rather than best-of) would just push the same
decision onto whatever consumes the score later without adding information; a
confidence-weighted combination was considered but rejected — averaging two genuinely different
archetypes back together is precisely what Sprint 4.8 designed the architecture to avoid
("never re-averaged back into one" — canonical doc §1), and doing so at the fit-scoring layer
would silently reintroduce the same problem one layer downstream. `profile_id` (A/B) of whichever
profile produced the best fit should be carried through to any future explanation layer, per the
canonical doc's own stated intent.

## Part F — Population diagnostics summary

- **Score/distance distributions**: reported per-method above (Part C); full per-position
  breakdown in `sprint5_2_distributions_by_position.csv` (99 rows: 9 methods × 11 positions).
- **Ranking correlations between methods**: `sprint5_2_method_agreement_summary.csv` — MAD vs.
  Euclidean/standardized-Euclidean/asymmetric-deficit ρ≈0.89–0.91 (largely interchangeable);
  MAD vs. hybrid ρ=0.83; MAD vs. centered-cosine ρ=0.79; MAD vs. shape-correlation ρ=0.66
  (genuinely different information); MAD vs. satisfaction-penalty ρ=0.56 (least reliable,
  highest variance across players — std 0.35).
- **Position-level behavior**: dimension coverage and score levels are consistent across all 11
  positions (no position shows a materially different MAD distribution shape) — position-level
  results are in the CSV, not separately tabled here since none showed an outlier pattern worth
  flagging beyond the already-known RM/LM WEAK-tier caveat (unchanged from Stage 4).
- **League-level behavior**: mean predicted-profile overall level varies only mildly by league
  country — Latvia/Finland/Iceland highest (≈51.7–51.8), Republic of Ireland/England lowest
  (≈49.1–49.3), a spread of 2.66 T-score points end-to-end. This is a mild, plausible
  sanity-check result (the CORE dimensions are already Competitive-Context-adjusted for league
  strength, so a *large* league effect here would have been a red flag; a small residual effect
  is expected and unconcerning).
- **Club-strength effects**: no separate club-strength variable was reintroduced (Context
  Ability / league strength is explicitly excluded per the Sprint 5.1 modeling contract) — the
  league-level check above is the closest available proxy and shows no strong signal requiring
  a correction.
- **Missing-dimension effects**: coverage is high and stable (median 11/11, 10th-percentile
  ≥10/11 for every position) — missing dimensions are not a material driver of any method's
  behavior at the population level, though individual players with several missing CORE
  dimensions should still carry a visible `n_dims_used` flag per the locked missing-data policy.
- **OBSERVED vs. inferred profile behavior**: mean MAD is nearly identical whether a candidate
  club is evidence-bearing (6.31) or fully-inferred (6.23) — fit *magnitude* does not
  systematically differ by evidence status, which is a reassuring finding (a future consumer
  shouldn't expect inferred-club fits to look systematically worse; `individual_reliability`
  communicates confidence separately from fit level, exactly as Stage 4 intended).
- **Individual reliability tier effect**: HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW tiers show materially similar mean MAD
  (6.20–6.37); only VERY_LOW is meaningfully higher (6.82) — consistent with VERY_LOW rows being
  driven by anomalous Team Environment input data quality (a modeling-confidence problem) rather
  than a systematically different profile shape.
- **PRIMARY vs. ALTERNATIVE**: covered fully in Part E.
- **Extreme/high-fit and extreme/low-fit examples, and disagreement cases**: `sprint5_2_
  disagreement_cases.csv` (275 sampled-player worst-disagreement rows between MAD and centered
  cosine) — the Aaron Morley case (Part D) is one of these, picked because it is football-
  readable and illustrates the exact risk the brief raised. Every disagreement case sampled
  showed a rank gap exceeding 30% of the candidate pool (275/275) — confirming this isn't a rare
  edge case but a systematic property of shape-only metrics whenever a player is scaled below (or
  above) a demanding profile while preserving its relative pattern.

## Part G — Recommendations

1. **Exact meaning of every usable Stage 4 profile**: OBSERVED = pure minutes-weighted
   descriptive mean of who's actually played there, no modeling, sample-size caveats fully
   disclosed. `predicted_<dim>` (System-Compatible) = Ridge(Team Environment) output, unblended
   with that club's own evidence except for the 255 two-profile cases (30% Ridge / 70%
   cluster-evidence blend). ALTERNATIVE = a second, equally legitimate archetype for
   evidence-supported heterogeneous cases only, never available for fully-inferred combos.
2. **Compatibility components that genuinely exist**: Observed Fit (evidence-bearing combos
   only) and System Compatibility (all combos, both profiles) are both legitimate and distinct.
   Squad Complementarity does not exist in Stage 4 and must not be fabricated from an existing
   column.
3. **Should Observed Fit and System Compatibility remain separate?** Yes — they answer different
   questions ("resembles who's played there" vs. "fits what the environment predicts"), have
   different coverage (72% vs. 100% of combos), and conflating them would hide exactly the
   circularity risk Sprint 5.1 flagged. Keep them as two distinct scores/inputs into any future
   combined Style Compatibility number, not merged upstream.
4. **Squad Complementarity recommendation**: defer as a separate future component (option 2),
   not removed from the roadmap and not silently repurposed from an existing Stage 4 field.
5. **Similarity methods — results**: MAD/Euclidean/standardized-Euclidean are largely
   interchangeable (ρ≥0.89) and each is a sound magnitude-based base. Raw cosine is unusable on
   T-scores (mean 0.989, negligible spread); centered cosine is meaningful but measures shape,
   not level, and disagrees with MAD often enough (ρ=0.79) to be dangerous alone. Shape
   correlation is the most different (ρ=0.66) and riskiest standalone. Requirement-satisfaction
   is the least stable (ρ=0.56, highest variance).
6. **Recommended mathematical definition of compatibility (for Sprint 5.3 to formalize, not
   locked here)**: a magnitude-based distance (MAD or standardized Euclidean — near-equivalent
   on this data) as the primary signal, with deficits weighted more than surpluses (the tested
   2×/1× asymmetric variant is a reasonable starting point, not a final calibration), computed
   only over shared non-missing dimensions with the count disclosed. Shape correlation may serve
   as a secondary, explanatory signal ("why" a match works) but should never independently
   override or dominate the magnitude-based primary score, given the demonstrated risk (Aaron
   Morley case) of shape-only similarity rewarding players who are simply scaled-down versions of
   what's required.
7. **Deficits vs. surpluses**: penalize deficits more than surpluses (asymmetric, not symmetric
   MAD). No evidence in this sprint that large surpluses need active penalization for this
   dataset, but the "very large surplus may signal a different archetype" question remains open
   and is a football-judgment call, not something this sprint's diagnostics can resolve
   unilaterally (see Part I).
8. **ALTERNATIVE archetypes**: best-fit-to-either (`best(fit(A), fit(B))`), never averaged,
   `profile_id` carried through to any future explanation — directly supported by the ~51%/49%
   split and the large (mean 31.9-point) separation between archetypes found in Part E.

### Unresolved questions / weaknesses (Part I, folded in for completeness)

- The deficit/surplus weighting ratio (2× tested here) is illustrative, not calibrated against
  any real outcome — there is currently no ground truth (actual transfer outcomes, agent
  feedback, etc.) to validate against, so any ratio chosen in Sprint 5.3 remains a reasoned
  default until such data exists.
- Whether extreme surpluses (worth investigating with a concrete magnitude threshold, e.g. >20
  points on a single dimension) represent "different archetype" rather than "better player" was
  not resolved — the population data alone can't distinguish these interpretations without
  independent football judgment or outcome data.
- Rank-based/percentile distance (Part C.6) was reasoned about but not run as a full separate
  population pass — flagged as a cheap, low-priority follow-up rather than decided against.
- Squad Complementarity's own methodology is completely undesigned — Part B only recommends
  *when* to build it, not *how*.
- Observed Fit for two-profile clubs has no per-archetype counterpart (only the combined,
  both-clusters Sprint 4.2 mean) — a structural gap in what Stage 4 currently outputs, not
  something Sprint 5.2 can fix without touching locked Stage 4 logic (explicitly out of scope

## Locked decisions (approved 2026-08-19, binding on Sprint 5.3 onward)

1. **Base distance metric: MAD**, not Euclidean — locked for interpretability (a direct average
   T-score gap) and robustness (no single extreme dimension dominates as it would under a
   squared-distance metric).
2. **Asymmetric MAD (deficits penalized more than surpluses) is locked as the framework**; the
   exact deficit/surplus ratio is explicitly NOT locked — to be selected empirically in Sprint
   5.3 from a tested range (1.25/1.50/1.75/2.00, extendable if diagnostics justify), using
   leakage-safe validation, not intuition.
3. **Squad Complementarity stays out of Style Fit entirely.** A related but distinct concept —
   **Conditional Alternative Opportunity** (player differs from the OBSERVED archetype but fits
   the SYSTEM-COMPATIBLE profile strongly) — is to be researched in Sprint 5.3 as a *separate*,
   conditional, non-forced signal, never merged into the main Style Fit ranking, with no
   thresholds locked yet.
4. **Shape similarity/correlation is diagnostic/explanatory only** — never enters Style Fit,
   never affects ranking, never a hidden weight. Retained to distinguish "meets the profile"
   (magnitude/MAD) from "is the same kind of player" (shape).
5. **Multiple OBSERVED archetypes: best-fit-to-either wins**, never averaged, PRIMARY never
   automatically favored over ALTERNATIVE. Fit against each archetype, the winning archetype,
   and per-archetype shape similarity must all be preserved in any future output.
6. **No separate Extreme Surplus Penalty.** Surpluses are already discounted (not ignored) by
   asymmetric MAD; a large positive gap is a Stage 6 (Level & Opportunity) question, not a Stage
   5 stylistic-incompatibility question. Stage 5 stays scoped to "does this profile fit how the
   club uses this position," not "is this level realistic."
